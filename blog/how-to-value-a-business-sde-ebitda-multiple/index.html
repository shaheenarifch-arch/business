What the business actually spits out for one working owner-operator. That's SDE. If you have a manager running it and you do not work in it, use EBITDA.

1. SDE vs EBITDA: Which Number Do You Use?

Think of SDE as the owner-benefit model and EBITDA as the investor model.

MetricSDE (Seller Discretionary Earnings)EBITDA
Best ForRevenue < $5M, owner-operator businesses, Main StreetRevenue $2M-$50M, manager-run, lower middle market
FormulaNet Income + Owner Salary + Owner Benefits + Interest + Taxes + D&A + Non-recurring + Non-business add-backsNet Income + Interest + Taxes + D&A + Non-recurring adjustments (owner comp NOT added back fully)
Owner SalaryAdd back 1 owner salary at 100%Do NOT add back. Replace with market-rate manager salary ($65k-$85k)
Buyer TypeIndividual buyer, SBA buyer will operate businessPrivate equity, strategic, search fund keeping management
2026 Multiple Range2.0x - 4.0x3.5x - 6.5x (for $1M+ EBITDA)

When to switch from SDE to EBITDA?

  • Rule: If SDE > $750k or business has a full-time GM who is not the owner and owner works <10 hrs/week, price on EBITDA.
  • SBA Lenders: Live Oak, Byline, Celtic underwrite on SDE for <$5M. If you pitch EBITDA under $750k SDE, you confuse the lender.
  • Conversion: EBITDA ≈ SDE - Market Manager Salary ($75k avg) - Owner Perks Already Counted. Example: $500k SDE - $80k manager = $420k EBITDA.

2026 Lending Tip SBA Form 1920 now requires add-backs >$10k to be documented with invoices. Keep receipts for every add-back you claim.

2. Recasting & Add-Backs: Turning Tax Return into True Cash Flow

Recasting is not cheating. It's normalizing the P&L to show what a new owner would actually make if they run it efficiently. Every broker and QOE firm does this. You need to do it cleanly.

The 8 Legitimate Add-Back Categories in 2026

  • 1. Owner Compensation: 1 owner’s salary, payroll taxes, health insurance, retirement contribution. If 2 spouses work, add back only one if buyer is solo. Need W-2 proof.
  • 2. Non-Business Personal Expenses: Personal car lease run through business, personal cell, family travel, kids on payroll who don't work, country club, etc. Must show bank detail that expense was personal.
  • 3. One-Time Non-Recurring: Lawsuit settlement 2024, building flood repair, COVID PPP consulting fee that won't repeat, one-time website rebuild $18k. Must be truly one-time - not "marketing" every year.
  • 4. Interest, Taxes, Depreciation/Amortization: Standard. Add back all interest, income taxes, D&A. For SDE, add back full interest even if you won't have same debt.
  • 5. Above-Market Rent: If owner owns building and charges business $9k/mo when market is $5k, add back $4k/mo = $48k add-back. Need market comp letter from CRE broker.
  • 6. Discretionary Non-Essential: Owner's $800/mo Tesla Model X lease classified as company car, personal Costco run $6k, charitable donations.
  • 7. One-Time Professional Fees: M&A advisor fee for deal that died, transaction attorney for failed acquisition.
  • 8. Extraordinary Covid / PPP: PPP loan forgiveness income - subtract it, but add back any extraordinary Covid losses if provable.

What Buyers and SBA Reject as Add-Backs

  • Future growth projections ("would have made $50k more if...")
  • Owner didn't take salary one year so profit looks high - lender will impute market salary
  • Meals & entertainment every week as personal - IRS allows 50%, but if it's client meals, buyer won't allow
  • Rent to yourself at market rate - no add-back
  • Inventory write-offs that happen every year - that's recurring

5. Negotiation Playbook: Defending Your SDE & Multiple

Buyer will always try 3 things: kill add-backs, push EBITDA salary, lower multiple due to risk. Here's how to respond.

Script 1: When Buyer Says "Those Add-Backs Aren't Real"

Buyer: "I don't agree that $27k legal is an add-back, looks recurring."

Your reply: "Totally understand. This was Smith vs Company 2024 slip-and-fall, settled Aug 2024, invoice #1847 from Attorney Johnson Law. Here's settlement agreement showing one-time. We haven't had litigation in 8 years prior. If you want us to exclude it, we keep net but also reduce price by $81k at 3x - your debt service drops $12k/yr. Your call, but we have documentation per SBA Form 155 standby guidelines. We can include it as add-back with invoice in data room."

Key: Never argue. Show invoice, settlement, and math of what removing does to price - buyer backs down.

Script 2: When Buyer Tries to Convert SDE to EBITDA With Full Market Salary

Buyer: "I need to hire manager at $95k, so EBITDA is only $328k."

Your reply: "If you're planning owner-operator SBA model, you replace my $95k salary with your own work, so SDE stays $423k - that's how Live Oak underwrites it. If you're absentee and need manager, you're right, EBITDA is $328k and priced at 4.2x EBITDA = $1.37M. That's similar to 3.25x SDE. We are comfortable either way, but let's pick lane: SDE 3.25x = $1.37M or EBITDA 4.2x = $1.37M. Both same enterprise value. Which financing are you using?"

Anchors value same, gives buyer choice.

Checklist: Pre-Due Diligence QA

  • Add-Back Binder: For every add-back >$2,500, keep 1 PDF: invoice, bank statement highlighted, explanation 2 sentences.
  • Proof of No Recurrence: 3 years GL showing expense only occurred once.
  • Market Comps: 3 similar BizBuySell sold comps at same multiple (download from BizBuySell Insights).
  • DSCR Calculator: Show bank: $423k SDE / $115k debt service (10yr 11.25% on $850k loan) = 3.67x DSCR = super safe.
  • Tax Tie-Out: 2023, 2024, 2025 tax returns Net Income + add-backs = SDE schedule. CPA letter confirming recast accurate.
  • Lease Abstract: One-page: expiry, rent, renewal, assignment clause, CAMs.

Final Formula to Quote

Enterprise Value = (Normalized SDE or EBITDA) x Market Multiple + Inventory (at cost) + FF&E if not in D&A + Working Capital Adjustment - Debt Assumed

Most Main Street deals: EV = SDE x 3.0 + inventory. Example: $423k x 3.2 = $1,353,600 + $35k parts inventory = $1,388,600 asking. Negotiation range 2.9x-3.4x = $1.226M - $1.473M.
