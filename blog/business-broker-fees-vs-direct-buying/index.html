1. Business Broker Fee Structures: Who Pays, How Much, and Why

If you are buying a business under $10M, you will encounter two worlds: Main Street brokers (BizBuySell, BizQuest listings, $100k-$2M revenue) and M&A advisors / investment bankers ($2M-$20M revenue). Their fees look similar but the math hits differently.

A) Who Actually Pays the Broker?

Seller pays In 95% of US transactions under $10M, the seller signs a listing agreement and pays the commission at closing from proceeds. As a buyer, you do not write a check to the broker unless you sign a Buyer Representation Agreement (rare on Main Street, more common with search fund advisors). However — indirect cost is real: seller nets need factor fee, so list price is inflated 8-15% vs off-market comp. When you buy direct, you can credibly say: "If we go direct, you save $80k broker fee, let's split it: $40k off price for me, $40k extra net for you."

  • Main Street (<$1M price): Seller pays 10% median, $120k business might pay $15k minimum.
  • Lower Middle Market ($1M-$10M): Seller pays 4-8%, success fee + $30k-$75k retainer upfront monthly.
  • Buyer-side advisor (Search / Independent Sponsor): You pay 2-5% if you hired an advisor to find deals. Negotiate cap and monthly off retainer.

C) Lehman Formula, Double Lehman, and Modern Lehman

Created by Lehman Brothers for large M&A, still used for $2M-$50M deals. It tiers commission down as price goes up.

Co-broker split: If buy-side broker brings buyer to sell-side broker's listing, they split 50/50 typically. Listing broker keeps 50-60% of fee, buy-side gets 40-50%. This is why many listing brokers prefer direct buyers — they keep 100%. Use it as leverage: "I am direct, you keep full commission, help me sell this price to your seller."

D) Hidden Broker Costs That Hit Buyers

  • Business valuation markup: Broker commissions are based on price, incentive to push 3.5x SDE when 2.8x is market. Always get independent valuation.
  • Inventory not included: Some brokers exclude inventory from enterprise value to lower commission optics, then add $150k inventory at close. Check: Does price include inventory at cost?
  • Success fee tail: Even if you go direct after seeing listing, if seller signed 12-month listing + 24-month tail clause, broker may still claim fee if you close within tail period. Ask seller: "When does listing expire? Is there a tail?"
  • Double dip on financing: Some brokers charge 3% financing fee if they arrange SBA loan. Decline — use direct SBA lender.

3. Off-Market Sourcing Playbook: How to Find 50 Owners Who Will Actually Reply

Off-market does not mean cold calling 500 random businesses. It means targeted outreach to 50 owners who statistically want to sell in next 12 months.

A) LinkedIn Outreach: Highest ROI Channel in 2026

Target profile: Owner age 58-72, title Owner/Founder/President, company 10-30 employees, in HVAC, plumbing, electrical, landscaping, MSP, auto repair, manufacturing. Why? Baby boomer retirement wave + no succession plan = 76% plan to exit in 10 years (Exit Planning Institute).

  • Search: Sales Navigator filter: Title = Owner, Company Headcount 11-50, Geography 50 miles around your city, Industry = Construction, Consumer Services, IT Services.
  • Sequence (3 touches): Day1 connection: "John - saw you built ABC Plumbing over 22 years in Worcester area. Impressive. Quick question about long-term plans?" Day3 if accepted: Voice note 28 sec: "John, not a broker — local operator looking to buy one plumbing company to grow, not flip. Not sure if you're considering exit in next couple years, but would love 15-min chat confidentially." Day7: Short email: "John - 3 bullets: Keep team, keep name, seller transition 6-12 mo paid. If not now, no worries."
  • Metrics: 60% accept rate if you have owner in headline, 18-22% reply rate, 8-10% call scheduled. So 50 outreach → 30 accepts → 6-7 conversations → 1-2 LOI opportunities.

B) Direct Mail to 50 Owners: Old School Still Works

Skip the list brokers. Build from Secretary of State + Google Maps. Letter MUST be handwritten yellow pad style, not typed flyer. Template:

Handwritten Mailer Template (52% open rate in tests):

Dear [First Name],
I live in [Town] and am looking to buy one [trade] business in [County] to run long-term, not flip.
I saw [Company Name] - 22 years in business is impressive.
If you ever thought about selling in next 1-2 years, I'd love a confidential chat. I can keep staff, keep your legacy, close fast (no broker fees).
Text me anytime: [Your Cell]
— [Your Name]
P.S. Not a broker. I am the buyer.

Mail 50 per week, not 500 per month. Follow up with call Day 7. Response rate: 6-9% callback vs 0.8% typed mail.

C) Trade Associations & Suppliers

The shortcut no one uses: Vendors know who wants to sell before brokers. HVAC supply houses (WinSupply, Ferguson), auto parts distributors, paint suppliers know because owner says "I might retire" while ordering.

  • Join local PHCC, NECA, ASA, chamber. Go to monthly lunch. Ask supplier: "Who in your accounts is thinking about slowing down?"
  • Offer $5k referral fee to supplier rep if deal closes (legal, not a broker fee - it's a marketing fee).
  • Conference tactic: Exhibit hall at AHR Expo, SEMA, Fabtech - 200 owners in one room.

D) Google Maps Scraping + Website Signal

Search: "plumbing company Worcester MA" → 68 results. Filter: Website last updated 2016-2020 (footer copyright), Facebook not posted in 6 months, owner 60+ by LinkedIn, 4.2 star but few recent reviews. These are tired owners. Build sheet: Company, Owner Name, Phone, Website age, Employee estimate, SDE guess via revenue (employees * $125k * 15% margin). Call: "Hi John, saw your shop on Google — still at 42 Main St? Quick question if you'd ever consider an offer."

Weekly Off-Market Checklist:

  1. Monday: Pull 50 new LinkedIn owners, send 15 connections
  2. Tuesday: Mail 15 yellow letters from last week's calls
  3. Wednesday: 1 trade association lunch + 2 supplier visits
  4. Thursday: 20 Google Maps calls (use Dialpad for recording)
  5. Friday: Log in CRM, follow up 7-day old outreach, set 2 calls for next week

Goal: 1 new LOI per month from direct, vs 1 per quarter from brokers.

4. LOI Tactics That Save You 15-30%: Price, Structure, and Contingencies

Letter of Intent is not binding on price, but it sets anchor. Most first-time buyers lose $50k-$200k because they LOI at asking price with weak contingencies.

A) Price Anchoring

Never LOI at list price. Broker list = wish price. Data: BizBuySell listings sell at 88% of list median after 196 days. Direct off-market sells at 78-82% of owner expectation. Formula:

  • Valuation: SDE * 3.0x for stable recurring, 2.5x for project-based construction, 3.5x for MSP/SaaS with contracts.
  • LOI Price: Start 15-20% below your target walk-away. Example: You will pay up to $900k for $300k SDE business (3x). LOI at $750k (2.5x). Expect counter at $950k, finish at $850k.
  • Justification line: "Based on 3-year average SDE $285k after owner add-back review, $750k reflects 2.6x plus $80k working capital inclusion and 6-mo transition risk."

B) Structure: How to Make Low Price Feel High

Script: "I'm at $750k total: $525k SBA cash at close, $150k seller note 6% 5yr, $75k earn-out if team stays 12 months. That's $525k Day 1, way more than you'd net after broker fee if you list."

C) Contingencies That Save Deals

  • Due Diligence: 45-60 days, not 15. Include books, customer calls (5), employee interviews, lease assignment, license transfer, QOE for >$1.5M.
  • Financing Contingency: "Buyer's obligation contingent on SBA financing at reasonable terms within 45 days." Without this, you lose $25k-$75k deposit if SBA declines.
  • Lease Contingency: Contingent on landlord assignment with at least 3-year renewal option at current rate + CPI.
  • Working Capital Peg: Define NWC = AR + Inventory - AP. Target = average last 12 months. Dollar-for-dollar adjustment at close. Prevents seller from draining cash last month.
  • Exclusivity Cap: Max 45 days exclusivity, not 90. Broker will ask 60-90, you counter: "We invest $8k in QOE and legal after LOI, so need reciprocity: 45-day exclusivity, after that LOI expires unless extended mutual."

D) Exclusivity Negotiation Script

Seller asks for 90-day no-shop:

"Happy to commit exclusively after you take it off market, but I will have $12k hard costs in week 2 for QOE and attorney. To keep momentum, 45-day exclusivity with auto-extension if SBA term sheet issued. If I can't get term sheet in 45 days, you keep deposit and relist. Fair?"

Works because you align incentives.

E) LOI Template - One Page Wins

Keep LOI to 2 pages max. Include:

  1. Price + Structure (cash, note, earn-out)
  2. Assets included (FF&E list, inventory, IP, phone number, Google Business Profile)
  3. Excluded assets
  4. Due Diligence list (8-10 bullet)
  5. Contingencies (financing, lease, license)
  6. Transition: seller 3 days/week for 3 months at $X/hr consulting
  7. Non-binding except confidentiality + exclusivity
  8. Close target: 60-75 days from LOI

Attach proof of funds letter from SBA pre-approval, not personal bank statement.

5. Closing Without Paying Twice: Negotiation & Fee Avoidance

Biggest mistake: LOI at $900k, seller says "broker fee on top." Always state in LOI: "Purchase price inclusive of all brokerage commissions. Seller responsible for payment per listing agreement."

A) How to Avoid Double Broker Claims

  • Ask seller upfront: Do you have listing agreement? When expires? Tail clause?
  • If listing active, have seller's broker sign fee acknowledgment at LOI: "Broker agrees commission payable solely by seller from proceeds, not buyer."
  • If off-market but seller talked to broker last 6 months, get written release that no tail applies, or escrow tail amount until tail expires.
  • Never sign buyer rep agreement for >30 days or >$10k fee without cap. Cross it out: "Buyer owes $0 unless closes with introduced party."

B) Final Negotiation Tactics Before APA

  • Re-trade responsibly: Only re-trade if diligence finds undisclosed: customer concentration +20% higher, SDE $50k lower, lease increase. Say: "We found 2 customers = 42% revenue vs 25% represented, that adds risk, need $70k price reduction or 15% earn-out."
  • Seller note as shield: If bank cuts loan 10% due to appraisal gap, ask seller to increase note 10% at same rate. "SBA will do $630k not $700k, can you carry extra $70k to keep $900k total?" 68% say yes.
  • Inventory audit Day -1: Physically count $120k inventory claimed. Short $25k? Reduce cash at close dollar-for-dollar.
  • Closing credits: Unfinished jobs, deferred revenue, PTO payout. Get credit: "Open WIP $90k cost already expensed, need $45k credit."
