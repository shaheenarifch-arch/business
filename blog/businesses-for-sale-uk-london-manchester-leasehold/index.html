Businesses for Sale UK 2026: London, Manchester, Leasehold vs Freehold, Share vs Asset Purchase

Freehold includes property and costs 2-3x more but protects against rent reviews. In 2026, the UK market for small business acquisitions is hotter outside London than at any point since 2019. Birmingham, Manchester, Liverpool and Leeds have seen a wave of retiring owners aged 58-68 listing cafes, HVAC firms, domiciliary care agencies, and B2B services. Unlike the US SBA model, the UK has no single government acquisition loan. Instead, you navigate leasehold premiums, Full Repairing and Insuring (FRI) leases, share vs asset tax treatment, and TUPE. This guide gives you the UK-specific playbook.

1. Where to Actually Find Businesses for Sale: London, Manchester, Birmingham

The UK has two dominant private marketplaces, plus broker networks and global aggregators. Knowing the city arbitrage is key.

London vs Manchester vs Birmingham Pricing Reality

London Zone 2-3: A £250k EBITDA coffee shop group leasehold might list at £750k-£950k (3-3.8x). Manchester Northern Quarter / Ancoats: Same EBITDA £600k-£750k (2.4-3x). Birmingham Digbeth / Jewellery Quarter: £550k-£700k and landlords are more flexible on rent-free.

  • London: High footfall, high rent (£30k-£120k pa for 1,000 sq ft), high staff turnover, but premium on exit. Best for hospitality, studios, clinics.
  • Manchester: UK's fastest SME growth outside London. Strong for digital agencies, trades (electrical, plumbing), gyms, and care. Landlords more open to assignment.
  • Birmingham / West Midlands: Manufacturing, light industrial, logistics, and automotive services. More freehold opportunities than London.

How to Search Rightbiz, Daltonsbusiness, and Beyond

Rightbiz.co.uk – Largest UK filter set: leasehold/freehold slider, accommodation included, turnover brackets. Set alerts for "relocation" or "retirement" – motivation reveals negotiation room.

Daltonsbusiness.com – Oldest UK portal (since 1870s). Strong for newsagents, off-licences, takeaways. Call the broker; many best listings are not fully described online to protect staff.

World Businesses for Sale (via Awin) – Aggregates UK + international for comparison. Useful when you are location-flexible or visa-linked. We recommend you cross-check valuations across regions.

Pro tip: UK listings often say "Leasehold: £95,000 + SAV". SAV = Stock at Valuation. Always ask for: unexpired lease term, break clause, rent review pattern (e.g., 3-year RPI), and whether landlord requires 3-6 months rent deposit + personal guarantee.

2. Leasehold vs Freehold vs Lease Premium: What You're Actually Buying

This is the #1 confusion for first-time UK buyers. In the US you buy assets; in the UK you buy a lease position.

What you getGoodwill, fixtures & fittings, right to trade from premises via lease assignment, stock at valueBusiness PLUS commercial property title. No landlord.
Upfront cashPremium (e.g., £40k-£300k) + SAV + depositPremium + property value (£250k-£2m+). SDLT payable
Ongoing costRent (£12k-£90k typical) + business rates + service charge + insuranceNo rent; only rates, insurance, maintenance. Mortgage possible
Lender viewLower security value (no bricks). Banks cautious if <5 years left on leaseProperty is collateral. High street banks prefer this

UK Lease Anatomy Checklist

Ask broker / solicitor for these 9 items:
  1. Term & Unexpired: You want >7 years unexpired. <5 years: negotiate extension or price drop.
  2. Rent Review: Open Market vs RPI vs fixed uplift. When is next review? Get landlord's intentions in writing.
  3. Assignment Clause: Does lease allow assignment? Is AGA (Authorised Guarantee Agreement) required? Does former tenant stay liable?
  4. Break Clause: Tenant-only break? 6 months notice? Penalties?
  5. User Clause: Can you change from cafe to bar? Some leases restrict A3/A4 use.
  6. Repairing Obligation: FRI means you pay even if roof fails. Get dilapidations schedule + building survey.
  7. Deposit & PG: Landlord wants 6 months rent deposit + personal guarantee? Budget it.
  8. Planning & Licensing: Is E-class correct? For alcohol, is premise licence transferable?
  9. Landlord Consent Timing: 2-4 weeks is normal. Don't exchange without written consent.

Negotiation script for landlord consent: "We understand you need a strong covenant. Buyer has [2 years accounts / funds / prior operator]. Happy to provide bank reference, 6 months rent upfront into rent deposit deed, and AGA limited to 12 months. Can we get Heads of Terms for assignment by Friday to keep seller's staff certainty?" Landlords fear voids more than anything; speed + security wins.

3. Share Purchase vs Asset Purchase: Tax, TUPE, and the EOT Wave

In UK, how you buy changes tax by six figures. This is not optional advice – you need both solicitor and accountant opinions.

IssueShare Purchase (buy company)Asset Purchase (buy trade & assets)
What you buyAll shares of Ltd company. Company stays same, you own itChosen assets: goodwill, equipment, stock, customer contracts. Leave company shell
LiabilitiesYou inherit ALL: HMRC, debts, lawsuits, skeletons. Need extensive warranties + tax deedGenerally you don't inherit. Clean break, except TUPE still applies
Employees (TUPE)Continues. Employment unchangedTUPE still applies by law if employees assigned to business transfer. You must consult
Tax for SellerBusiness Asset Disposal Relief (10% CGT up to £1m lifetime) if conditions met – very attractiveSeller pays Corporation Tax on profit + then extract cash. Often higher effective tax
Tax for BuyerNo SDLT on business, 0.5% stamp duty on shares (>£1k). Can't claim capital allowances on old assets again. Amortisation of goodwill restrictedPays SDLT/LBTT if property included. Can claim capital allowances on fixtures/equipment. Goodwill amortisation still tricky
Why chosen in 2026Sellers demanding share sale, especially Employee Ownership Trust (EOT) unwinds, retirement salesBuyers' solicitor preference (70% of SME deals under £1m are asset deals)

TUPE – The Non-Negotiable People Law

If you buy a UK business where employees are assigned to its operation, TUPE (Transfer of Undertakings (Protection of Employment) Regulations 2006) automatically transfers their contracts to you on existing terms. You cannot "pick and choose". You must:

  • Inform and consult with staff (or their reps) in writing at least 2 weeks before transfer
  • Honour continuity, holiday accrual, pay, and no dismissal because of transfer
  • Take due diligence: copy of contracts, length of service, any tribunal claims, pension auto-enrolment

Failure to consult = up to 13 weeks pay per employee penalty. Budget 2-3 hours with an employment solicitor.

The EOT Trend You Can't Ignore

Since 2020, 2,000+ UK companies moved to Employee Ownership Trusts due to 0% CGT for sellers. In 2026, many are selling non-core divisions or the EOT itself is selling (CGT advantage already used). When you see "EOT-owned, profitable, £1.2m turnover", ask: are trustees sellers? Is valuation HMRC-cleared? Expect share sale, independent trustee approval, and longer DD.

BUYER NEGOTIATION SCRIPT – Asset vs Share Price Adjustment Seller: "We want £500k for shares." Buyer: "We prefer asset purchase because we can't take historic HMRC risk. Based on share sale, £500k net to you ≈ £450k after BADR. For us to do asset purchase, your corp tax + dividend tax would be higher. Can we bridge? Either: A) £460k asset price + £40k consultancy/earn-out over 12 months to manage tax, or B) £500k share price but with £75k held in escrow 12 months against warranties/tax covenant, plus full disclosure of HMRC enquiry history. Which works to keep you near your net?" — This frames tax equivalence, not just headline price.

4. UK Due Diligence Playbook: Companies House, Lease Assignment, and No Surprises

US buyers lean on QofE. UK SMEs need leaner, smarter checks.

Step-by-Step Checklist (4 Weeks)

  1. Week 1 – Companies House & HMRC: Pull filing history on Companies House Beta (free). Check PSCs, charges (mortgages), overdue accounts. Ask for CT600s, VAT returns, management accounts last 24 months. Cross-check: does turnover in listing match accounts? Any HMRC Time to Pay?
  2. Week 1 – Lease: Get copy lease, land registry title, rent & service charge demands last 12 months, EICR, Gas Safe, EPC (>E illegal to let). Instruct solicitor to ask landlord for consent in principle.
  3. Week 2 – Commercial: 12-month sales by channel, top 10 customers concentration, supplier terms, Google reviews, key person dependency (owner does 50% sales?). Visit at different times – Monday morning vs Saturday rush.
  4. Week 2-3 – Employees & Compliance: Full payroll, contracts, handbook, pension provider, H&S risk assessments, food hygiene 5 rating if F&B, GDPR data audit if you inherit database.
  5. Week 3 – Legal: Asset Purchase Agreement (APA) or Share Purchase Agreement (SPA) – warranties, disclosure letter, restrictive covenants (6-24 months non-compete reasonable). For leasehold, assignment + rent deposit deed + licence to assign.
  6. Week 4 – Funding & Completion: High street bank offer letter, insurance (public liability £2-5m), premise licence transfer, merchant acquirer transfer.

Red Flags Specific to London/Manchester

  • London: Business rates revaluation + ULEZ delivery surcharges + rent review 40% jump post-Covid boom.
  • Manchester: Many retail leases 2021-22 had Covid rent concessions now ended – check actual contractual rent vs paid.
  • Both: Service charge cap? Some Manchester mixed-use have 10-20% annual cap, London often uncapped.

Offer & Negotiation Framework That Works in UK

UK sellers get 3-7 enquiries, 1-2 serious. Speed + proof of funds beats small price bump.

Your Letter of Intent (LOI) Should Include:
  • Price and structure: "£X total, £Y on completion, £Z deferred 12mo, £W stock at valuation"
  • Lease term requirement: "Subject to assignment of lease with minimum 7 years at rent not exceeding £"
  • Funding: "Funded by cash + bank facility – bank term sheet expected within 14 days"
  • Conditions: "Due diligence 3 weeks, landlord consent, no material adverse change"
  • TUPE acknowledgement and consultation plan
  • Exclusivity 21-28 days
UK NEGOTIATION SCRIPT – After DD finding a £25k dilapidations risk "Thanks for disclosure. Surveyor flagged potential £25k dilapidations (photos attached). We still want to proceed. Two options to close this week: Option A) Reduce premium by £18k and we take risk, or Option B) Keep price but £20k held in retention for 6 months post-completion pending landlord confirmation no Section 10 claim. Seller keeps interest on retention. Which do you prefer? We can get landlord consent letter today if we agree." — Anchors solution, not blame. UK brokers respond to this.

Cost Budget (UK average): Solicitor (buyer) £4k-£8k asset, £8k-£18k share. Accountant review £1.5k-£5k. Broker fee paid by seller (usually 8-10%). Landlord legal fee £750-£1,500 payable by you. Insurance transfer £600-£2k. Always hold £5k contingency.

Closing? UK uses simultaneous exchange & completion for small deals (no gap). Funds via solicitor client account, TR01 if shares, assignment Deed if lease. Update Companies House PSC, bank mandates, HMRC, insurance, and suppliers on Day 1. Keep seller for 2-4 week handover – paid consultancy.

Final Thought: London gives prestige and liquidity; Manchester/Birmingham give margin and landlord goodwill. Leasehold is fine if unexpired term is healthy and rent review is known; freehold is equity fortress but ties capital. Master share vs asset + TUPE and you de-risk 80% of failed UK deals. Do the boring Companies House + lease + payroll trinity first, then fall in love with brand.
